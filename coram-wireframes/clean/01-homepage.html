What this website can and can't do

  • Shows organisations and places that created care and adoption records, and who holds them now
  • Explains how to ask for your records and what your rights are
  • It does not hold any records
  • A directory entry does not confirm that your own records exist

If you can't find an entry, it doesn't mean your records no longer exist.

This website contains information about children's experiences of care, adoption and family separation, including historical practices that may have caused harm. Some of it may be upsetting. Find support at any time.

Useful to have to hand

You can search without any of these. But they often hold the clues that lead you to the right place.

Your birth certificate

It can show where you were born and where your birth mother was living at the time. Search for those places. If you were adopted, you'll usually need it when you ask for your records.

Getting a copy of your birth certificate

Papers from your time in care

Letters, reports or other documents you were given, which may name a council, home or social worker's office.

What you or your family remember

Names of places, homes or organisations, and rough dates, even a decade. Write them down before you start.
